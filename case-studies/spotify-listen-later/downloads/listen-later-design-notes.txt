SPOTIFY LISTEN LATER
An independent product design exploration by Joel Jojy, 2026.

PURPOSE
Save released songs and whole albums for a future listening session.
The concept covers Spotify mobile and web, with a collection in Your Library.

SAVING
- A gray outline clock beside Add to Your Library saves an item to Listen Later.
- A green clock means the item is saved. Tapping again removes it.
- The existing three-dot menu also contains Save to Listen Later.
- Existing Spotify controls and menu actions are preserved.
- A confirmation offers Undo. Saving does not change current playback.

COLLECTION
- Listen Later appears beside Liked Songs in Your Library.
- Songs and albums share one list, with All / Albums / Songs filters.
- Recently saved is the proposed default order.
- Gray clock + Not started: listening has not begun.
- Horizontal progress bar: partial album or song progress.
- Green tick + Listened: completed or manually marked as listened.
- Status icons sit beside labels, never on album artwork.
- Albums remain single collection items.

PROPOSED BEHAVIOR TO VALIDATE
Prevent duplicate saves; keep items until removed; support manual listened /
unlistened status and account-level cross-device sync. Define completion
thresholds through research. Brief previews should not complete an item.

DELIVERABLES
Three PNG design boards and a 40-second 3840 x 2160, 30 fps walkthrough.
The walkthrough follows the menu route and displays the quick-save control.
The boards separately show the unsaved and saved quick-clock states.
Screens were created through an AI-assisted design workflow and refined
against user-provided Spotify interface references. Displayed play counts
and progress values are illustrative mock data, not research results.

LIMITATIONS
These are raster concept mockups and an animated walkthrough, not a working
Spotify integration, interactive prototype, editable Figma file or production
implementation. No usability testing, research findings, measured impact,
live playback tracking or cross-device synchronization is claimed.
